# Zoomable paper: an interactive reading demo

A demonstration of how a journal article could be read at different lengths, depths, audiences and points in time.
Open it on an iPad in Safari and use your fingers on the text.

- Pinch out or in: longer or shorter, for the whole paper. A meter appears on the right edge.
- Swipe a paragraph left or right: more technical or plainer, for that paragraph. A slider appears under it.
- Double-tap, or twist two fingers: move through time, from publication in 2021 to 2026. Each double-tap adds the next year of evidence to every paragraph; in summaries the closing sentence updates to the newest finding. On a computer, double-click, circle two fingers on a trackpad, or drag the dial.
- Reading as: Researcher, Clinician, Policymaker or Public, in the header or the glass panel.
- Reader insights: a simulated layer for authors and journals showing where readers zoom, simplify and stop.
- On a computer: trackpad pinch and two-finger swipe, or the keys + − ← → , . 1–4 and i. Press 0 to return to the published text.

The article is Reid M, Abdool-Karim Q, Geng E, Goosby E (2021) *How will COVID-19 transform global health post-pandemic? Defining research and investment opportunities and priorities*. PLoS Med 18(3): e1003564. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1003564. It is reproduced under the Creative Commons Attribution License (CC BY 4.0).

The published text is the centre position of the reader: Researcher, full length, as published. Every other version was generated by an AI model from the published text. References 14 onwards were found with Consensus and checked against PubMed in November 2026; they are not part of the article, and the time dial hides any published after the date you choose. Reader insights use simulated data.

This is not a PLOS product. Inspired by an interaction concept shared by Fabian (@fabrohl).

A one-minute teaser video is at [teaser.mp4](https://vanheerdena.github.io/zoomable-paper/teaser.mp4). Its narration and music are AI-generated (Google Gemini TTS and ElevenLabs Music via fal.ai); the reader statistics shown are simulated.

## Second demo: a research article with methods, results and tables

[vanheerdena.github.io/zoomable-paper/jias/](https://vanheerdena.github.io/zoomable-paper/jias/) applies the same reader to Gati Mirembe B, et al. "High recent PrEP adherence with point-of-care urine tenofovir testing and adherence counselling among young African women: results from the INSIGHT cohort". J Int AIDS Soc. 2024;27(12):e26389, reproduced under CC BY 4.0.

What is new here:
- Tables and the flow figure are zoomable units: swipe a table for a plain view (icon arrays, natural frequencies), the published table, or a technical view (forest plot of adjusted and unadjusted ratios, model specification, sensitivity bounds, site-level supplement data).
- Tap an underlined statistic to see what it means, where it comes from and what to keep in mind.
- Every number in every generated version is checked against the article, its supplement and the evidence pack; values derived from published counts are labelled DERIVED and appear only in appendix-level detail.
- Section headlines and summaries: at the shortest lengths each section collapses to one sentence.
- The time dial covers December 2024 to November 2026 in half-year steps, with 28 later sources verified in PubMed.
