# Zoomable paper: an interactive reading demo

A demonstration of how a journal article could be read at different lengths, depths, audiences and points in time.
Open it on an iPad in Safari and use your fingers on the text.

- Pinch out or in: longer or shorter, for the whole paper. A meter appears on the right edge.
- Swipe a paragraph left or right: more technical or plainer, for that paragraph. A slider appears under it.
- Twist two fingers: move through time, from publication in 2021 to 2026. Clockwise adds later evidence; anticlockwise takes it away. On a trackpad, circle two fingers, or drag the dial.
- Reading as: Researcher, Clinician, Policymaker or Public, in the header or the glass panel.
- Reader insights: a simulated layer for authors and journals showing where readers zoom, simplify and stop.
- On a computer: trackpad pinch and two-finger swipe, or the keys + − ← → , . 1–4 and i. Press 0 to return to the published text.

The article is Reid M, Abdool-Karim Q, Geng E, Goosby E (2021) *How will COVID-19 transform global health post-pandemic? Defining research and investment opportunities and priorities*. PLoS Med 18(3): e1003564. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1003564. It is reproduced under the Creative Commons Attribution License (CC BY 4.0).

The published text is the centre position of the reader: Researcher, full length, as published. Every other version was generated by an AI model from the published text. References 14 onwards were found with Consensus and checked against PubMed in November 2026; they are not part of the article, and the time dial hides any published after the date you choose. Reader insights use simulated data.

This is not a PLOS product. Inspired by an interaction concept shared by Fabian (@fabrohl).
